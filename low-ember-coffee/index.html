Batch No. 014 — Ethiopia Guji, in the hopper now

Why low and slow

on roasting

Most roasters push heat hard and fast to hit a batch quota. We do the opposite. Every batch spends longer in the drum at a lower temperature, so the sugars develop gradually instead of scorching on the surface.

It costs us time and it costs us yield. What it buys back is a cup that tastes like where it came from — the stone fruit still there, the florals still there — instead of a cup that mostly tastes like roasting.

We cup every batch before it goes on the shelf. If it doesn't taste like the farm, it doesn't go out the door.
